Brand · The look

Data

Green carries, terracotta flags. A khyzr chart says one thing at a time, and reads as khyzr before its legend does.

Green carries. Terracotta flags.

Green is always the point: the first series, the number that matters. Everything else supports it. One terracotta flag marks the thing that must be seen.

  • Green takes the first series, always. No chart leads with another color.
  • Cobalt compares. Steel gives context.
  • Terracotta is loud on purpose and rare on purpose: one flag to a chart, and never a series.
  • On the deep green, cobalt sinks. Series run green, steel, steel tint. Terracotta still reads, for marks.
  • Past four categories, regroup the data.

The cast.

One lead, one comparison, two for context, one signal.

  • khyzr Green: the first series. Lines are drawn in Forest over a khyzr Green fill.
  • Cobalt: the second series. Last year, the benchmark, the comparison.
  • Steel: the third series, and the ghost of a comparison: last year's dashed line behind this year's green.
  • Steel tint: the fourth series, the remainder, "everything else".
  • Terracotta: the flag. A negative, an annotation, the one value to see.

How a chart is drawn.

Soft, rounded, and editorial. A chart is set like a page, not like a dashboard.

  • Bars have rounded ends. Axes are single hairlines. Fills under a line fade away to nothing.
  • Values are set in Outfit, semibold, and labeled at the end of the line or bar where they can be. Skip the legend box when the labels do its work.
  • Axes, legends, and captions are small labels in Manrope.
  • No pies, no donuts, no 3D, no shadows, no heavy grids.
  • On a screen a chart arrives once: bars grow from their baseline and numbers count up. Nothing loops.
  • PowerPoint cannot round a bar. In a deck, bars keep square ends, and every other rule here holds.

Two good examples.

A comparison over time, and a set of groups with one flag.

  • A trend. This year is the green line. Last year is steel, dashed, and labeled at its end. No legend box.
  • Grouped bars. Green leads each group and cobalt compares. The one value to see swaps its cobalt for terracotta.

One number, set large.

When a number is the point, it gets the room: an ink numeral at display size, its unit in green beside it, and a small label underneath.

  • One number to a slide or a panel.
  • Say what it counts underneath, and what it means beside it.

What not to do.

A flag is not a failure, and a color is not a decoration.

  • Don't run terracotta as a series, or twice in one chart.
  • Don't lead with cobalt or steel, or show either without green.
  • Don't use the color of an error in a chart. Errors live in forms.
  • Don't split one total as a pie. Use one rounded bar, the largest slice in green.
  • Don't set Bright leaf in a chart. It lives in the gradient only.
