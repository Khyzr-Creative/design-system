Brand · Who we are

Voice

How khyzr sounds and how its copy is written. The personality, the eight rules, how a sentence lands, the mechanics, the words, and the name.

Who is speaking.

Approachable. Transparent. Dependable. The brand looks enterprise-formal; the person you meet in the writing is human. First person "we", direct "you". No exclamation marks, no hype adjectives, no emoji, ever.

The three words, glossed

  • Approachable. We write the way we talk across a table.
  • Transparent. We say what we see, and what we don't know yet.
  • Dependable. We promise only what we will do. Then we do it.

The eight rules.

  1. Approachable, transparent, dependable, in every sentence.
  2. Talk like a human. Plain, conversational sentences. No copywriter punctuation: no em-dash constructions, no colon-heavy headlines.
  3. Empathetic, never accusatory. The buyer's past choices were reasonable.
  4. Concrete over metaphor. Say what actually happens: "the work comes back in separate pieces from separate teams," not "silos."
  5. Calm and assured, never scrappy or underdog. khyzr has reach; write like it.
  6. Forward-motion language. The brand moves things; sentences should too.
  7. Never frame the relationship as ending. No "when we're done," no "we leave you with." Permanence is earned, not declared, so we never declare it either; we just never write the goodbye.
  8. Candor as integrity. We tell you what we see, not what you want to hear.

Why each rule holds

RuleWhy
1These three words are who we are. A sentence that shows none of them could be anyone's.
2We look formal on purpose. The writing is where you meet the person.
3Hiring a big name was the reasonable move. We say so, and start from there.
4Our category speaks in big nouns. We make concrete promises instead.
5The reader has a lot at stake. They should feel covered, not sold to.
6What a client should remember is a business moving forward. A sentence that sits still argues against it.
7Work has phases. The relationship stays open.
8Integrity is the root. Candor is how it sounds.

How sentences land.

The signature moves.

  • The turn. The sentence ends on a reversal: "…what we see, not what you want to hear." One turn per paragraph, at most.
  • The callback. One phrase may recur per page, as an echo, not a chorus.
  • The reassure. Where an action asks for someone's time, one line beneath it lowers the stakes: "A short conversation to see if we're the right fit." Ask, then soften.
  • Long–short rhythm. A flowing sentence, then a clipped one: "It's never just one thing." The short sentence carries the weight.
  • Two-beat headlines. Period-terminated pairs: "Every discipline. One team." · "Strive Forward. Thrive Together." The second beat gets the green word.

Mechanics.

  • The action. Each brand has one primary action, worded one way everywhere. khyzr says "Book a conversation". khyzr.ai and khyzr.studio say "Get started". Never "Learn more", "Contact us" or "Book a demo", on any of the three. A text link starts with a verb and carries the arrow: "See the work", "Visit khyzr.ai".
  • Casing. Sentence case everywhere, headlines included; the tagline keeps its own casing. A headline ends with a period. Uppercase exists only in small labels.
  • Punctuation. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no ellipses, and none of the copywriter's em-dash constructions. Middle dots (·) join taxonomy labels: "Finance · Accounting".
  • Labels. Small labels are uppercase and tracked, in the technical face, never a monospace. They never set a sentence. Lanes read like a taxonomy, not a tagline: "Healthcare AI", "Brand · Marketing · Design".
  • Claims. Confidence without superlatives about ourselves. We describe what we do and let restraint carry the status; the copy never begs.
  • The tagline. "Strive Forward. Thrive Together.", the carried verbal asset, closes and signs the © line. "Everything starts here." is the house's second line: it opens, it is never called a tagline, and it never sits beside the tagline. Don't coin new taglines per page.
  • Credit. Partners and clients get factual credit, never co-branding: "In partnership with X.", "Built with X.", "Work with X.", "X is a khyzr client." Never "khyzr × X", "Powered by khyzr" or "An official X partner". Never "former client" or "past client": the relationship is not written as finished. The marks' side of the rule is on the Logos page.

  • "Alongside your team", not "inside yours," not "for you."
  • Completion is the client's felt outcome, never outbound copy. We never tell a client their business is incomplete without us.
  • The promise is the whole board, not "anything for anyone."

The name.

Al-Khidr, "the Green One": الخضر

The name is told once per surface, with its story: the sage people sought when the path wasn't clear. An advisor, not a ruler. He gave counsel, and the credit stayed with the people he helped.

  • One name moment per surface: the Arabic name appears once, and only in that moment, set in Noto Naskh Arabic. On a product, that moment is its copyright line.
  • Never as decoration, never sprinkled through copy.
  • Browser and app chrome are exempt.
  • Elsewhere, the company is simply khyzr, always lowercase, and so are its products: khyzr.ai, khyzr.studio. That holds in copy, in titles, and in the names of files and folders; code and the GitHub organization keep the spelling they were made with. Never "Khyzr", never "khyzr Consulting Group".
  • A product is named khyzr.[x] and carries the masterbrand. No new brand names.
  • What the name means is on The brand page.

How the names are written

  • Lowercase holds at the start of a sentence and in a headline.
  • Today that is two products: khyzr.ai and khyzr.studio.
  • A product's name is written as it is addressed: lowercase, one dot, no space. Never "khyzr AI", never "Khyzr.ai", never "khyzr Studio".
  • Inside a product, things take plain names and no mark of their own. khyzr.ai's agents are named for what they cover: Finance, Operations, Security.
  • The color is written khyzr Green.

The style sheet.

It settles what the record left mixed.

Spelling

Three sites and every document should read as one company, so we spell one way.

  • American English: color, center, organization, program, license, gray, traveled, and the -ize endings.
  • The serial comma, always: "growth, health, and wealth."
  • Al-Khidr takes its hyphen.
  • Another company's name, product, or headline keeps its own spelling: Amazon Bedrock, not "AWS Bedrock".

Punctuation

The rules ban the copywriter's dash, and a rule with exceptions gets argued, so we use none.

  • No em dash anywhere. A period, a comma, or a colon does its work.
  • An en dash only for a range in a table or a label: 11–13px. In a sentence, write "to".
  • No exclamation marks, no emoji, no ellipses.
  • Middle dots (·) join taxonomy labels: "Finance · Accounting".
  • Double quotation marks, single inside them.
  • "and" in a sentence. "&" only in a label or in a name that owns one: Sales & Marketing.

Numerals

Numbers are proof, so they should be easy to read and hard to misread.

  • One to nine in words. 10 and up in figures.
  • Always figures with a unit, in money, in percentages, in sizes, and in data: 3%, $3,900, 24px, a 30-minute call.
  • A number never starts a sentence as a figure. Write the word, or turn the sentence.
  • Thousands take a comma: 1,020. The percent sign and the unit sit closed up: 12%, 24px.
  • A leading zero belongs to the numerals of a numbered list, in the technical face: 01, 02. Never in a sentence.
  • A proof number is set large, with its unit beside it and a caption in the technical face.

Dates and times

A date should read one way to everyone, wherever they are.

  • Day, month, year, the month in words: 5 October 2026. No "th", no commas.
  • In a table, the short month: 5 Oct 2026. Never all figures, since 5/10 and 10/5 read two ways.
  • Month and year alone where the day adds nothing: October 2026.
  • Brand files carry no date and no version, in their names or on their pages. A letter, a proposal, and a contract are dated, because they are records.
  • Times on the 12-hour clock: 9:30 am. Name the time zone when readers are in more than one.

Titles and headings

Sentence case reads like a person. Title case reads like a form.

  • Sentence case everywhere, headlines included. That covers slide titles, document titles, and buttons.
  • Uppercase belongs to small labels only.
  • A headline ends with a period. A card title, a table head, a label, a button, and a menu entry do not.
  • No colon-heavy headlines.
  • A file or a folder is named in plain words that begin with khyzr, lowercase, with no date, no version, and no "final".
  • A job title takes capitals in a signature, on a card, and in a list of names. In a sentence it is lowercase: the creative director at khyzr.
  • A discipline takes a capital as the name of a card or a label: Strategy. In a sentence it is lowercase.

Abbreviations

We talk like a human, and humans say the whole thing first.

  • Spell it out the first time, then shorten: accounts payable (AP).
  • What every reader knows stands alone: AI, CEO, COO, IT, US.
  • No periods inside an abbreviation: US, am, pm.
  • "for example" and "that is", never "e.g." and "i.e.". Never "etc."
  • khyzr is never shortened and never an initial.

The call to action

Each brand asks for one thing, so each has one primary action, worded one way everywhere.

  • khyzr starts with a conversation. A product starts with use.
  • One primary action on a surface. The two wordings never share one.
  • On a khyzr surface, a product is reached by a link, not a button: "Visit khyzr.ai".
  • Never "Learn more", "Contact us", or "Book a demo", on any of the three.
  • A second action is a text link that starts with a verb: "See the work".

Tone by channel.

The voice does not change. Length, formality, and how much proof you show do.

ChannelHow it flexes
A deckOne claim to a slide, as its title, in sentence case with its period. The slide proves the title. No paragraphs: say them aloud. One number, large, where a number is the point. The last slide is the tagline, then the one action.
A proposalOpen on their problem, in their words. Then what we will do, who will do it, what it costs, and what happens first. Name the work, the people, and the dates. Say what we would not do, and what we don't know yet. Write phases, never an ending. "We" and "you", never "the Client".
LinkedInThe first line is the point. One idea to a post. Write as a person at khyzr, not as a press office. No emoji, no exclamation marks, no wall of hashtags. Credit clients and partners in the credit wording.
EmailThe subject says what the email is for. Open on the point. One ask, stated plainly, with its date. Short paragraphs. Sign with your name and the khyzr signature, whichever product you work on.
